Our Lodge

We are the heirs to a legacy that spans generations. From the founding of the first Grand Lodge in London, England in 1717 to the founding of the first Masonic Lodge in Whittier over 125 years ago. Masons have worked to support their communities and each other. Looking over the history of our country and the world, many men who have been instrumental to the founding of nations, invention and discovery, government, learning and the arts have been members of our craft. We can look back with pride at who we have been and what we have done.

But we also look forward. It is our duty to proceed in the footsteps of those who came before us and continue the legacy of action for the betterment of our communities and the world. Masons today strenuously promote a quality free and public education because an informed citizenry is the backbone of democracy. Masons gather together to promote morality and virtue and improve themselves so that they can be exemplary in their life and conduct. We live with the hope that we can, through works great and small, leave the world a better place than we found it.

We are Free and Accepted Masons of the State of California.

Become a Freemason

1

When you become a Mason you become a member of the world’s oldest fraternity. You become a part of a history that spans hundreds of years. You become part of an organization with lodges all over the world. You become part of a legacy that has included millions of honorable, faithful and vital men who made their communities better places in ways big and small. You become a brother to laborers, police men, architects, builders, scientists, artists, presidents and kings.

Masons are charged with being an example to other men. Masons live their lives in a moral and ethical manner. Masons take care of their families. Masons support the law. Masons have reverence for God. Masons support those in need whether they be other Masons or members of the community at large. Masons, having been brought into the light, work tirelessly to share the light with the world.

2

Your local Masonic lodge does not, has never and will never recruit anyone to join. Every Mason that has ever been has first selected himself to be a member of our society. Once you have made up your mind to join us, the next step is to ask. Every lodge meets at regular intervals. Most meet on a weekly basis. All you have to do is come to the lodge and talk to our members.

3

Our own lodge meets on Thursday nights. The first Thursday of every month we hold our monthly business meeting and dinner. Dinner begins at 6:30 and ends when we begin our business meeting usually around 7:30. This is the best time to drop in, meet our members and get to know more about us and what we do. All other Thursdays we meet to plan and prepare for events. If you visit us, one of our members will be happy to speak with you and answer any questions you might have.

4

First off don’t be shy. Fortune favors the bold. If you can be guaranteed of one thing it is that Masons are by definition good men so there is no reason to be afraid. Most Masons will happily go out of their way to tell interested people about masonry and what it means to them. Just walk up to someone and tell them that you are interested in learning more about Masonry and that should start the ball rolling.

Remember that there are many lodges across southern California each with its own distinct character and membership. You should always be mindful that the lodge you apply to is a place where you feel comfortable and can grow as a man and as a Mason. The lodge will definitely be making sure that you are a good fit as a candidate so it is only fair that you make sure that the lodge is a good fit for you.

5

Prospective Masons must profess a belief in God but we do not discriminate based on religious preference. There are Masons from every major religion and sect thereof and plenty that do not belong to any organized religion at all.

Prospective Masons must be good men. All Masons submit to a criminal background check and are investigated by the lodge at which they apply for membership. The application requires that you provide two sponsors who are already members of the lodge you wish to join, two personal or professional references and background information about employment and residency.

6

Application fees vary by lodge but most are in the range of few hundred dollars. Application fees must be presented along with the application in order for the application process to begin. Once you have been initiated, there are annual dues that must be paid to remain a member in good standing. Those dues also vary from lodge to lodge. Application fees, except for the portion to pay for the mandatory background check, are refundable if your application is not accepted.

7

A criminal background check will be conducted and your application will be read at one of our monthly meetings. Once it is read a panel of three members will be selected to investigate your application. They will call and talk to your references and recommenders and will make an appointment to interview you in person, preferably at your home. Based on the information the collect they will issue a report as to your suitability for membership. If the background check is passed and the committee makes a favorable report your application will be voted on at one of our monthly meetings. If you are elected, you will be notified of your acceptance and a date will be set for you to be initiated by receiving your first degree.
